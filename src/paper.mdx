    <div><p className="chapter-kicker">02 / Motivation</p><h2 id="perspective">Different views.<br />{" "}Different roles.</h2></div>
    <p className="chapter-intro">A wrist camera can reveal an insertion point that the global view cannot see. As the robot moves, the most useful view changes with it.</p>
  </header>

  <Figure fullWidth>
    <Picture slot="figure" src={teaser} alt="An occluded placement scene motivates preserving local camera detail and learning each view’s contribution to action prediction." />
    <Fragment slot="caption">Complementary views, unequal relevance. FlexWAM preserves local visual detail, exchanges context across cameras, and learns how much each view contributes to the current action.</Fragment>
  </Figure>

  <div className="research-takeaway">
    <h3>Preserve detail.<br />{" "}Learn where to look.</h3>
    <p>Tiling camera images into a fixed canvas can sacrifice the detail needed for precise manipulation. FlexWAM encodes each view separately, alternates within-view and cross-view attention, and makes camera contributions explicit through observation-dependent routing.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section className="research-section not-prose" aria-labelledby="method">
  <header className="chapter-heading">
    <div><p className="chapter-kicker">03 / Method</p><h2 id="method">Learning to route<br />{" "}with counterfactual credit.</h2></div>
    <p className="chapter-intro">Action loss alone can leave camera weights nearly uniform. <strong>Counterfactual (CF) view credit</strong> gives the router a direct learning signal: how much does action prediction depend on each view?</p>
  </header>

  <figure className="method-framework">
    <header>
      <h3>Mask a view. Measure what changes.</h3>
      <p>Counterfactual view credit measures the change in action loss when one visual branch is masked. A critic learns normalized credit and calibrates the router.</p>
    </header>
    <Picture src={framework} alt="FlexWAM framework: view-aware world modeling, counterfactual view credit, and the view-routed action expert." />
    <figcaption>The critic supervises routing during training; observation-dependent view weights guide action generation through View-Routed Action Attention.</figcaption>
  </figure>

  <div className="research-takeaway">
    <h3>Learn view credit.<br />{" "}Route from the observation.</h3>
    <p>The critic and counterfactual evaluations are used only during training. At deployment, the router predicts camera weights once per observation and reuses them across action-denoising steps.</p>
  </div>

</section>
